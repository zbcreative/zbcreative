Salons, Spas, and Wellness Businesses: The Website Features That Actually Book Appointments

By Zack Balzer·Jan 28, 2026·9 min read

Your clients are scrolling Instagram for inspiration, but when they're ready to book, they're Googling "best salon near me" or asking ChatGPT "where should I get a facial in [city]." The beauty and wellness industry runs on referrals and reputation, and your website is where both of those things live online.

Most salon and spa websites look nice but don't actually work hard enough. A pretty homepage with a "book now" button isn't enough anymore. Your website needs to show up when people search, communicate what makes you different, and make booking seamless. Here's what that looks like across 18 types of beauty and wellness businesses.

Hair and Nail Services

Hair salons, barbershops, nail salons, and blowout bars are the high-frequency services that clients return to regularly. Your website needs to convert first-time visitors and keep regulars coming back.

Hair SalonsBarbershopsNail SalonsBlowout BarsKids SalonsMobile Stylists

Hair salon websites should showcase stylist profiles with their specialties, portfolio images, and individual booking links. When someone searches "balayage specialist near me" or "curly hair salon in [city]," the salons with stylist-specific content win those searches. Service menus with pricing in text are essential since AI can't read a pricing PDF.

Barbershops should lead with services, pricing, and walk-in vs appointment availability. "Barbershop near me" is a massive search category, and the shops with clear hours, location, and services in structured data show up first. Before-and-after galleries of cuts and fades with text descriptions let AI index your work.

Nail salons need service menus organized by category (manicures, pedicures, acrylics, gel, nail art) with pricing. Health and sanitation practices should be prominently featured since that's a top concern for new clients researching online.

Spa and Skincare

Day spas, medical spas, estheticians, and skincare clinics serve clients making considered purchases. These aren't impulse visits. People research treatments, compare providers, and read reviews before booking.

Day SpasMedical SpasEstheticiansSkincare ClinicsMassage TherapyCryotherapy & Recovery

Day spa websites need treatment menus organized by category (facials, massage, body treatments, packages) with pricing and duration. Gift card integration is a major revenue driver that many spas bury too deep in their site. Seasonal packages and couples experiences should have prominent placement.

Medical spas sit at the intersection of beauty and healthcare. Provider credentials matter enormously here. Board certifications, medical director information, and before-and-after galleries with procedure descriptions build the trust that converts researchers into patients. Treatment pages for Botox, fillers, laser treatments, and body contouring each need dedicated content.

Massage therapy websites should clearly describe modalities offered (deep tissue, Swedish, sports, prenatal, Thai) since clients search for specific types. Online booking with therapist selection and session length options removes friction from the conversion process.

Wellness and Body Services

Wellness centers, tattoo studios, permanent makeup artists, tanning salons, teeth whitening, and waxing studios round out the beauty and wellness landscape.

Wellness CentersTattoo StudiosPermanent MakeupTanningTeeth WhiteningWaxing Studios

Tattoo studios live on their portfolio. Artist profiles with style specializations (traditional, realism, watercolor, blackwork, fine line) and galleries organized by style help clients find the right artist. Booking process, deposit information, aftercare content, and FAQ pages answer the questions first-time clients always have.

Permanent makeup artists need before-and-after galleries with detailed descriptions, healing timeline content, and certification information. "Microblading near me" and "permanent eyeliner [city]" are high-intent searches where portfolio quality and trust signals determine who gets booked.

Wellness centers offering holistic services should clearly describe each modality and its benefits. Practitioner bios with certifications and training help both clients and AI understand your qualifications.

What Every Beauty and Wellness Website Needs

Across all 18 beauty and wellness business types, the essentials are the same: service menus with pricing in text, online booking integration, provider or stylist profiles with portfolios, before-and-after galleries with descriptions, Google Business Profile optimization with current photos, structured data marking up your services and reviews, and an FAQ section covering booking policies, cancellations, and what to expect at first visits.

The beauty businesses that invest in their websites stop relying solely on Instagram for new clients and start showing up in the searches that actually drive bookings.
